BRIGHAM D. Madsen, emeritus professor of history at the University of Utah, was born in Magna, Utah, and raised in Pocatello, Idaho. After two years at the Southern Branch of the University of Idaho-now Idaho State University-he spent two years on a mission for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints most of the time _in the Cumberland Mountains of eastern Tennessee. Of that experience he recently wrote: "In 1934, many were still living as their ancestors had done in the days of Daniel Boone. I entered into their lifestyle, was fascinated by their language and dialect, enjoyed their 'singings,' and resolved to investigate and to learn more about their origins and background. It was a principal reason why, later in my graduate work, I majored in Colonial American history."[^1]

Madsen completed his undergraduate education as a history major at the University of Utah and then spent a year as the principal of the school at Pingree, Idaho, a few miles west of Blackfoot. From Pingree he went to the University of California, Berkeley, and there completed his Ph.D. with time out for service during World War II.

Madsen's university-level teaching career began at Brigham Young University and continued at Utah State University and the University of Utah; an eighteen-month break in the mid-1960's took him to Washington, D.C., with the Peace Corps and VISTA. Despite substantial teaching loads and the supervision of graduate students, for which he gained a Distinguished Teaching Award from the University of Utah in 1977, he has consistently maintained a pattern of research and writing, principally on topics related to the history of the Great Basin. Madsen is the author of nine books (one co-written with his wife, Betty), editor of five more, and has also written a number of journal articles and reviews over the years.

In both teaching and writing, Madsen is committed to the art and craft of narrative history. "If told well," he says, "the story will interpret itself."[^2] Certainly his story of The Bannock of Idaho is testimony to his own gifts as a narrative historian.

[^1]: Brigham D. Madsen, The Craft of History (Salt Lake City, Utah: The Utah Westerners, 1995), 2.

[^2]: Ibid., 16.
